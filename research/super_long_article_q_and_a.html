1. Executive summary

Short answer: not as the default. The sample article is 26,687 words / ~55k estimated tokens of text (~86.7k tokens once split into the app's per-paragraph trunks). The model's native window is large enough on paper, but the answer call in this application uses num_ctx = 32,768, so the whole article does not fit. Even after raising the window, stuffing 55k tokens into every turn is slow, expensive, and gives worse answers than targeted retrieval because of context dilution and “lost in the middle.”

The article can be answered well without ever putting all of it in the prompt. The existing /article implementation already contains the right primitives: a compact overview trunk (728 estimated tokens), a bounded digest trunk (2,881 estimated tokens), and 2,089 paragraph trunks that are retrieved with BM25. The missing pieces are a clearer routing policy, section-level expansion, and an iterative mode for questions that require evidence from several distant parts of the document.

The three recommended approaches, in order of value for this codebase:

  1. Structure-aware hierarchical summarization with a question router — precompute an overview, a document digest, and per-section summaries; send broad questions to summaries and narrow questions to retrieved paragraphs. This is the smallest change and should be the default.
  2. Retrieve-then-rerank with parent-section expansion — retrieve many candidate paragraphs cheaply, rerank with the small model, then expand each winning paragraph to its enclosing section. Best for narrow, factual, and multi-hop questions.
  3. Iterative agentic retrieval with a sufficiency loop — plan sub-questions, retrieve in small batches, ask a small model whether the evidence is sufficient, and stop when it is. Best for “list every…” or “compare sections…” questions, at the cost of extra model calls.

So the honest answer to “can I fit the entire article in the context?” is: not with the current answer settings, and not with a meaningful quality guarantee even if you raise them. The model's 262k ceiling is a capability, not a quality target. Long-context quality is usually degraded well before the hard limit, and the degradation is uneven: models tend to use the beginning and the end of the prompt much better than the middle. Long-context benchmarks also typically measure retrieval of a planted sentence, not reasoning over an entire technical document.

What happens if we raise num_ctx to 65,536?

The plain text would fit; the full trunk set would not. Three other costs appear immediately:

  • Prefill latency. Time-to-first-token grows with prompt length. A 55k-token prompt must be processed before the first output token; on a local machine that is a noticeable pause on every turn, unless the prompt is cached and unchanged.
  • Memory. KV-cache memory grows roughly linearly with context length. The exact size depends on layer count, KV heads, head dimension, and quantization. Multi-GB KV caches are expected at this length; verify with ollama ps before committing to it as a product default.
  • Answer quality. A 55k-token prompt is mostly irrelevant to a narrow question such as “what is the retention window for a removed tag?”. Retrieval quality often improves when the prompt is 2–8k tokens of highly relevant evidence instead of 55k tokens of everything.
Practical rule: use long context as a fallback for small documents and as a last synthesis step over retrieved evidence. Do not use it as the first-line retrieval strategy for a document that is larger than the retrieval budget by an order of magnitude.

4. Why “just put the whole article in the prompt” is the wrong default

  1. Context dilution. Every retrieved paragraph competes with thousands of unrelated paragraphs for the model's attention. Narrow answers become less precise and more likely to mix sections.
  2. Lost in the middle. Long prompts are used unevenly; evidence placed in the middle of a large context is recalled less reliably than evidence near the start or end (Liu et al., Lost in the Middle, 2023).
  3. No citations. If the whole document is one blob, the model cannot tell the user which section supports a claim. The current app's per-paragraph trunks solve this; a single blob loses it.
  4. Cost per turn. Every follow-up question re-sends the whole document unless prefix caching can reuse it. Even with caching, a changed question or system preamble can invalidate the cache.
  5. Refusals and hallucination both increase. When the relevant evidence is buried, models either claim the context is insufficient or fill the gap from parametric memory. The first behavior is visible in the bug reports that motivated this research; the second is worse because it is silent.
  6. Hard failure for bigger documents. The sample is already at 55k tokens. A 200-page HTML report or a book-length document breaks the strategy entirely, whereas retrieval degrades gracefully.

5. Three ways to get high-quality answers

Strategy A — Structure-aware hierarchical summarization + question router recommended default

Build a small summary tree when the article is loaded, then route each question to the right level of that tree. 

Article
├── Document overview        title + outline + TL;DR/executive summary   (~700 tokens)
├── Document digest          overview + 1 excerpt per major section      (~3k tokens)
├── Section summaries        one 150–300-word summary per H2              (~100–300 tokens each)
└── Paragraph trunks         one knowledge piece per paragraph            (retrieval unit)

How it answers questions

  • Broad questions (“what is this about?”, “summarize in 1000 words”, “what are the main recommendations?”) are answered from the overview or digest, not from BM25 paragraphs. Generic words such as “what”, “this”, and “about” otherwise match arbitrary paragraphs.
  • Narrow questions (“what is the retention window?”) go through normal BM25 retrieval over paragraph trunks, with the small-model relevance filter and token budget already implemented.
  • Section-level questions (“explain the audit process”) retrieve the best paragraph, then attach its section summary and its neighboring paragraphs so the answer has local coherence.

Why it works

  • The summary tree is built once per content hash and can be cached on disk (the current code already hashes the path + bytes into app/.article_cache/).
  • The router is cheap: the existing article_overview_question() recognizes broad/deictic phrasing, and word-count requests are already parsed.
  • Every answer level keeps provenance: overview/digest are synthetic trunks, paragraph and section chunks still map back to headings.

Cost and limits

  • Summary generation needs one pass over the article with a local model (map: section summaries; reduce: document digest). Do it at load time and cache it. A failure must fall back to the current extractive overview so /article never blocks.
  • Summaries can lose specifics. Narrow factual questions must still be answered from raw paragraphs, not from summaries.
  • For documents without usable headings, fall back to fixed-size windows with overlap and summarize each window.
Best fit for this app: it extends the existing overview/digest design instead of replacing it, keeps the current BM25 path, and fixes the most common failure mode (broad questions receiving unrelated paragraphs) without increasing per-question prompt size.

Strategy B — Retrieve-then-rerank with parent-section expansion

Treat paragraph trunks as leaves and headings as parents. Retrieve broadly, rerank aggressively, then send only the most useful local neighborhood. 

question
  → query expansion / keyword extraction          (small model, optional)
  → BM25 over all paragraph trunks                (top 50–100)
  → small-model relevance filter                  (keep 10–20)
  → rerank by query–paragraph relevance           (cross-encoder or LLM)
  → expand each winner to its section:
       previous paragraph + heading + next paragraph
  → deduplicate and enforce a 6–10k token budget
  → synthesize with citations to paragraph/section IDs

Why it works

  • Retrieval recall is separated from prompt budget: you can inspect many candidates without sending them all to the 35B model.
  • Parent-section expansion fixes the “orphan paragraph” problem. A paragraph that says “the default is 30 days” is useless without the heading that says which retention period it refers to.
  • Neighbor expansion helps multi-hop and definitional questions without pulling in a whole section of 20k tokens.

Cost and limits

  • An extra reranking call per question; keep it on qwen3:1.7b or a lightweight embedding/BM25 hybrid to avoid latency.
  • Requires a reliable heading-to-paragraph map. The current extractor already collects headings; it needs to record each paragraph's section span during _load_article_chunks().
  • For questions that deliberately span many sections (“list every retention period in the document”), local expansion is not enough; use Strategy C.

Strategy C — Iterative agentic retrieval with a sufficiency loop

Instead of deciding the context once, let the system search, check, and search again. This is the same idea as chat_gradual_context.py, adapted to article retrieval. 

plan = decompose(question)                   # 1–5 sub-questions
evidence = []
loop:
    for each uncovered sub-question:
        new = bm25(search_terms(sub-question), exclude=already_seen)
        new = rerank(new)[:3]
        evidence += new
    if estimate_tokens(evidence) > budget: break
    verdict = small_model(
        "Given these excerpts, can every part of the question be answered "
        "with a specific fact or quote? Answer YES or NO and name missing parts."
    )
    if verdict is YES: break
    search_terms = expand_from_missing_parts(verdict)
synthesize(question, evidence)               # with citations

Why it works

  • It handles multi-part and enumerative questions (“list all levels”, “compare X and Y across the document”) that a single top-k retrieval cannot cover.
  • The small model only judges sufficiency; the 35B model is called once for the final synthesis, which keeps output quality high.
  • A hard token budget prevents runaway context growth. A typical stopping point is 8–16k tokens of evidence, far below the 32,768 window.

Cost and limits

  • Multiple small-model calls per question; latency grows with the number of iterations. Cap iterations (e.g., 4) and token budget (e.g., 16k).
  • Sufficiency prompts can be overconfident. Require a cited evidence snippet in the verdict, not just “YES”.
  • Risk of looping on a genuinely absent answer. After the iteration cap, answer from what exists and explicitly say which part is unsupported (without the current blanket refusal).

Long-context fallback (not a primary strategy)

If the document is small (say, under ~20k estimated tokens) and the user explicitly asks for a whole-document answer, a single long-context call can be acceptable: raise num_ctx, keep the overview at the start and the question at the end, and stream the answer. This is a special case, not the default. For the sample article it would require at least a 64k window and would still be slower and less precise than Strategies A–C.

6. Recommended routing by question type

Question shape Examples Context source Target evidence budget
Broad summary / “what is this about” “Summarize this article”, “what is this about in 1000 words?” Overview (short) or digest (long/detailed) 0.7k–3k
Narrow factual “What is the default retention period?” Top paragraph(s) + heading + neighbors 1k–4k
Section explanation “Explain how the audit process works” Section summary + best paragraphs in that section 3k–8k
Enumerative / comparative / multi-hop “List every tag type and its retention rule” Iterative retrieval over sub-questions 8k–16k
Whole-document, small doc “Rewrite this 8k-token spec as a checklist” Full text in one call, num_ctx raised ≤ 20k

Two guardrails apply to every row: never exceed the answer window, and always keep citation IDs so the user can see which section a claim came from.

7. How this maps onto the current code

Already implemented

  • app/chat_server.py
    • _load_article_chunks() decodes HTML/Markdown, extracts title and headings, splits paragraphs, content-hashes the cache, and writes one trunk per paragraph.
    • _article_overview() builds chunk 0 from the title, outline, and TL;DR/summary.
    • _article_digest() builds chunk −1 from the overview plus the first substantive content of each major section (capped at ARTICLE_DIGEST_CHARS = 16,000).
    • article_overview_question() routes broad/deictic and word-count questions to the overview or digest.
    • Chunk −1 is excluded from ordinary BM25 so its headings cannot crowd out paragraph matches.
  • chat.py
    • answer() retrieves paragraph trunks with BM25, applies the qwen3:1.7b relevance filter, enforces the MAX_CONTEXT_TOKENS budget, and streams the final answer with ornith-1.5:35b at ANSWER_NUM_CTX = 32,768.
    • _select_auto_chunks() already implements an elbow, diversity, per-video cap, and token-budget stopping rule. For article mode, “per-video” is effectively “per-document”, so the cap should be revisited if one article dominates retrieval.

Highest-value changes

  1. Persist a section map. During _load_article_chunks(), record for every paragraph its nearest preceding heading and its section span. This enables parent expansion (Strategy B) and precise citations.
  2. Cache generated section summaries and the digest. The existing cache directory is content-addressed, so summaries can live beside the trunks. Build extractive summaries first (fast, deterministic) and upgrade to model-written summaries only if quality demands it.
  3. Make the router explicit. Extend article_overview_question() into a small classifier with three labels: overview, section, narrow. Keep the current regex rules as the fallback when the classifier is uncertain.
  4. Add parent/neighbor expansion to the narrow path. After selecting the top paragraph(s), append the heading and one or two neighboring paragraphs, then re-check the token budget.
  5. Add an opt-in iterative mode. Reuse the loop design from chat_gradual_context.py, but cap iterations at 4 and evidence at 16k tokens. Enable it automatically for enumerative phrasing (“list all”, “every”, “compare”, “across the document”).
  6. Report real token usage in benchmark mode. The current estimate is character-based. For tuning, log Ollama's reported prompt/eval token counts so the budget and summary sizes are based on real tokenizer numbers.

8. Evaluation plan

Build a small, fixed question set for the sample article (20–30 questions) and score each strategy on the same machine. Do not judge by one anecdotal question.

Question classCountExampleWhat “good” means
Broad summary5“What is this article about in 500 words?”Covers the main sections; no invented facts
Narrow factual8“What happens to a tag when it is removed?”Correct fact + correct section citation
Definitional4“What is a product tag in this system?”Answer grounded in the document's definition
Enumerative4“List all tag audit checks.”High recall of distinct items
Multi-hop3“How does tagging affect search ranking, and where is that enforced?”Correctly combines two or more sections
Unanswerable3“What is the pricing of the tagging service?”Says the document does not contain it; no hallucination

Metrics

  • Faithfulness: every claim is supported by a cited excerpt (human or LLM judge with the excerpts in the prompt).
  • Answer coverage: fraction of key points present for enumerative and summary questions.
  • Citation accuracy: cited section actually contains the supporting fact.
  • Time to first token and total latency: measured with the app's existing timing hooks / /benchmark.
  • Prompt tokens per answer: real Ollama counts, not only the character estimate.
  • Refusal rate on answerable questions and hallucination rate on unanswerable questions.

9. Risks and failure modes

  • Summary drift. Model-written summaries can omit the exact number or exception the user later asks about. Keep raw paragraphs as the retrieval source of truth; summaries are only for broad questions.
  • Heading detection is heuristic. HTML headings, numbered sections, and Markdown headings are handled, but documents that use styled <div>s or PDF-like layouts may have no usable structure. Fall back to fixed windows with overlap.
  • One giant paragraph. A paragraph longer than the retrieval budget cannot be used whole. Split it by sentence groups before trunking.
  • Multiple documents. The current per-video cap in _select_auto_chunks() is designed for transcripts. In article mode it is effectively a per-document cap; verify it still behaves when several articles are loaded in one session.
  • Cache invalidation. The content hash covers path and bytes, so edited articles get fresh trunks. If summary generation becomes model-dependent, include the model name and prompt version in the cache key.
  • Privacy and cost. All strategies are local, but iterative modes multiply local inference calls. Cap loops and expose the number of retrieval rounds in benchmark mode.
